The question behind the experiment

Why Spanish?

Because sometimes the things we put off are not dead ends. They are doors we have not yet learnt how to open.

This page deliberately retains the Latin Experiment’s structure while the personal account is still being written. The Spanish Experiment began with the discovery that an old direct-method book could make a surprising amount of Spanish readable from the very first pages.

Poco a Poco does not begin by drowning the learner in labels. It uses pictures, repeated questions, short answers and connected Spanish to let meaning establish itself naturally.

Spanish became interesting not because an application counted a streak, but because the original text began to make sense.

A language beside other languages

Spanish belongs to the same wider Romance family as French, Italian, Portuguese and Latin. Shared roots are useful, but the experiment treats Spanish as a living language in its own right rather than merely as a descendant of something else.

The final personal account can be rewritten later without changing the page design or navigation.

Not an app-hopping project

The aim is to test one coherent public-domain course properly: reading, handwriting, speaking, listening, conversation and honest reflection, rather than accumulating disconnected exercises across several platforms.

What I am hoping to find

I want to know whether Hall Avilés’ direct method can still produce useful, conversational Spanish for a modern British learner who works through it carefully and supplements it with real speakers and modern listening.

This website records that attempt in public. The mistakes matter. The awkward first readings matter. So do the moments when a pattern suddenly becomes obvious.
